---
title: The First Foray
permalink: /2026-10-26/
---
Under thunderous and angry skies, our intrepid party ventured forth: **Sir Reginald Faetoucher** (Minor Noble, Lvl 1), **Jeremy Pines** (Chinampas Farmer, Lvl 1), **Balthazar S. Hammer** (Miner, Lvl 1), and **John Don-Geon** (Dock Worker, Lvl 1). Approaching the reportedly cursed and haunted ruins of the Tomb of Gidim, they entered a structure coated with greenery. A mighty spiral staircase lay before them, flanked by pillars. Upon the north wall beyond the staircase was a message written in glyphic - the writing system of the ancients - and though indecipherable, the manner in which it was written seemed ominously threatening.<br>

Descending down the stairway, the party came across a room with four exits: north, east, south, and west. Heading north, they traversed a section of corridor carpeted by moss. There were rooms immediately to the west and north, with doors damp and swollen in their frames, stiff to open. The room to the west contained moss peeling off the walls. The room to the north contained a strange plant that began to grow exponentially in light. After fighting with the plant for a period to no avail, the party opted to shield their torch under a cooking pot and clear the way in near darkness. They wedged an iron spike in the top of the door behind them, in order to serve as an audible warning should anyone open it.<br>

Beyond the plant lay a door west, leading to a twisted corridor. As the party continued westward, they heard a strange rhythmic hissing noise ahead. Discovering a door to the south, Jeremy Pines tried prodding it with a wooden pole. The door opened shortly after, and the party found themselves face to face with five wary lizardmen. As soon as the party adopted an aggressive stance, the lizardmen sprang into action, splitting their forces and engaging at the choke point in the doorway. After an exchange of blows, John Don-Geon was mercilessly skewered, and had his jugular ripped out. The party opted to flee, though Jeremy Pines stayed behind to avenge John Don-Geon. Sir Reginald Faetoucher and Balthazar S. Hammer reached the winding corridor to the immediate east just in time as the split forces of the lizardmen closed in for a flank attack behind them. Jeremy Pines managed to bisect the lizardman that killed John Don-Geon, before fast succumbing to the piercing blows of the lizardmen and being left for dead.<br>

Back in the overgrown room, Sir Reginald Faetoucher and Balthazar S. Hammer used their torchlight to regrow the plant to block any potential pursuit, all the while hearing the sickening sound of snapping bones and ripping flesh, signalling the demise of Jeremy Pines. There was a clank as the iron spike wedged in the door fell, and four frogmen opened the southern door. Making himself "look big", Sir Reginald Faetoucher scared off three of the four froglings somewhere to the south, and the fourth promptly gave up. As they took a rest, two more figures arrived: **Julius Pines** (Gourmand, Lvl 1) and **Jonathan Don-Geon** (Hermit, Lvl 1). Deeply saddened to hear they had arrived moments too late to save their relatives, they nonetheless steeled themselves for the expedition ahead.<br>

Jonathan Don-Geon drew a copy of the glyphic text in the entranceway, with the aim of getting it translated later. The party encountered a curious and friendly giant centipede, whom they named "Cat". Travelling through the mossy room to the west and into the corridor beyond, the party stumbled upon a section of the floor that was of a lighter colour than the floor preceding it. Closer examination revealed six holes in the north wall, in two columns of three. Stepping in between these openings, Julius Pines triggered the spear trap. As long as his weight remained on the pressure plate, the spears remained extended. Sir Reginald Faetoucher utilised his crowbar to pry off the lower row of two spears, and the party were able to crawl underneath.<br>

The party found a room with four levers, oxidised with age. They actuated one of the levers with a horrific screech, and a mechanical sound was heard somewhere to the west. Though unsure of what they may have released, the party pursued the source of the sound, and discovered a series of three cells, two of which were blocked with portcullises. These cells were strangely empty, but the sound of simian grunting lead the party to discover a fourth cell, larger than the rest. Within was an angry apeman, violently shaking the bars. The apeman was given a lit torch, which he promptly threw to the back of his cell. He was then given a full waterskin, which after some suspicion he drank in a matter of seconds. Returning to the lever room, the party oiled the levers, then actuated them to open all three of the remaining cells.<br>

The freed apeman thanked the party, and beckoned them to follow to the south. The party were led down a winding series of corridors, eventually discovering the apeman was not alone; there were a group of apemen exploring the ruins, seeking a new home. Though there was a signifiant language barrier, the apemen managed to communicate that they had been skirmishing with the lizardmen to the north, and that the apeman they had just freed had been knocked out. The party were then lead to a room covered in cave paintings not native to the tomb, and a spiral staircase that lead up towards an entrance to the complex in the south-west, well hidden by the dense foliage. The party hacked their way to the main trail, and headed back towards civilisation.
